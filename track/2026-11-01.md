## Key ideas

- **Why IDs instead of positions.** "Insert at index 5" breaks when someone else inserts at index 2 at the same moment. "Insert after character 7@bob" means the same thing on every replica, forever.
- **Lamport clock.** Every new op gets a counter one higher than anything the replica has seen. So if Alice saw Bob's character before typing, hers has the higher counter. Sequential typing always sorts correctly; only truly concurrent edits need a tie-break.
- **The document is a tree.** Each character is a child of the character it was inserted after. Reading depth-first gives the text. A new character goes right after its parent, so it is placed before older siblings.

## Bug caught by tests

The first version appended new characters after their older siblings, so inserting "!" after "h" in "hi" gave "hi!" instead of "h!i". `TestInsertInTheMiddle` and `TestInsertAtStart` caught it. Fix: a new character goes first among its siblings.

## Known gap

Siblings are ordered by arrival. Two replicas that receive concurrent inserts at the same spot in different orders can disagree. Fixed on Nov 2 by ordering siblings by ID.
